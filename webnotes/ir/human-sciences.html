Human Sciences

Origin

The human sciences are quite old endeavors. The fields of grammar, logic, and rhetoric were studied in the middle ages at the University of Bologna. But they were seen as techniques and instructions more so than a field of study. This shifted during the Renaissance of humanism in the 16th century. That process formalized the human sciences and continued through to the 19th century.

Fields of the Human Sciences

  • History
  • Art History
  • Linguistics
  • Literary Studies
  • Philosophy
  • Religious Studies

The world studied by the human sciences is made up of historical human actors. These actors are creative, from them originates acts, texts, works of art. This creation does not follow rules but instead is conceptualized as unpredictable and inexplicable.

Acts Example: Caesar crossing the Rubicon

Caesar crossing the Rubicon was an act. The act originated from him. The same can be said for the creation of a work of art.

The human sciences zoom in on historical particularity (much the inverse of the Natural Sciences). Each event and its context is regarded as wholly unique. The human sciences identify eras such as the renaissance or enlightenment, but these act only as taxonomical categories for subjects of study to be placed into, they are not accompanied by any general rule for that period.

The human sciences have an intrinsic distrust for generalizations or idealizations. The highest for of knowledge in these fields is intimate knowledge of the particular: the life and acts of a certain figure, or even acts at a particular time. There is little to no use for the prospect of scientific laws and as such the human sciences are especially oriented around the Idiographic Approach.

Interpretations

If laws of nature are the most impressive format and the core output of the Natural Sciences then the human sciences' core output is interpretations. Interpretations of acts, texts, and artworks are often embedded in theoretical frameworks through which there is an attempt to reconstruct the historical actor's world of experience and meanings. These interpretations can then be tested against the text or other material of that actor. When generated thoroughly the interpretation can be considered quite objective.

Some complications

The groups of disciplines are more diverse and complicated than may be described. The picture given is of course a simplification. While natural sciences do generally value laws, this is less valuable in biology, cell biology for example where mechanisms take priority. In the human sciences, fields such as linguistics and archaeology use some methods inspired by the natural sciences. Marxist historiography for example go against the human sciences distrust of generalization, believing in universal laws of historical development.

Honors Programme · Pre-Thesis Research

Institutional Attenuation

Thematic Seminar: FDI, MNCs & Emerging Economies · 2026 · 5,388 words

Introduces the concept of institutional attenuation: the structural process by which democratic tax systems absorb a predictable fraction of statutory corporate tax rates before they translate into effective corporate burdens. OLS regression across 59 democracies found that for every percentage point increase in the statutory rate, effective rates rise by only 0.94 points. Spearman correlation (ρ = 0.370, p = 0.004) confirmed the attenuation gap grows significantly with country size, reframing the race-to-the-bottom debate and analyzing the OECD's Pillar Two as a constraint on this mechanism.

Global Political Economy

This Dictator Does

2025–26 · Essay

Uses Singapore as a deviant case to challenge Genschel et al.'s (2016) argument that autocracies have no structural incentive to compete for foreign capital via low corporate tax rates. Argues the theory fails because it conflates regime type with legitimacy source: Singapore's PAP grounds its authority in economic performance, functionally paralleling a democracy's electoral incentive to attract capital. Proposes revising the unit of analysis from regime type to legitimacy source for a more robust theory of tax competition.

Mozambique / FRELIMO

2025–26 · Research Paper

Applied process tracing across 1962 to the late 1980s to explain why FRELIMO established a one-party authoritarian state despite its origins as a broad anti-colonial coalition. Found that both competing explanations hold simultaneously: militarized institutional legacies created the authoritarian state structure, which was then reinforced and expanded as a rational response to genuine security threats from RENAMO and foreign adversaries.

German Ordoliberalism and the Hartz Reforms

2025–26 · Essay

Examined whether Germany's Hartz I-IV welfare reforms under Chancellor Schröder constituted a departure from Ordoliberal welfare logic. Surveyed three competing academic explanations, modernization, neoliberal shift, and pragmatic crisis response, concluding that all three perspectives have merit and that the reforms represent an ongoing academic debate about the nature of institutional change in Germany.

Tyranny: Ancient Histories and Modern Paradigms

Tyranny of a God: Dionysus and the Discourse of Tyranny in Euripides' Bacchae

2025–26 · Essay · 6 pages

Argues that Dionysus in Euripides' Bacchae exhibits the traits of the túrannos as understood in late Archaic and early Classical Greece, drawing on Luraghi's framework of recurring characteristics through which tyrannical behavior was recognized. Read against the historical tyrant Pisistratus, Dionysus' extralegality, cunning, marginal social position, and mobilization of women against the established order reveal a figure who, without holding official office, wields and relinquishes power entirely on his own accord.

Introduction to International Studies

South Africa: Energy Inequality & the Green Transition

2024–25 · Final Report

Examined energy insecurity in South Africa as a lens into systemic post-Apartheid inequality, finding that white South Africans consume 47% more energy than the rest of the population despite being 10% of it, and that coal provides 82% of supply while being deeply tied to employment of historically disenfranchised Black citizens. Proposed a balanced green transition that phases out coal jobs through retraining and diversification while expanding nuclear, solar, and hydroelectric generation, presenting South Africa as a potential blueprint for the African continent.

Comparative Politics

Cuba's 2019 Constitution: Freedom and Equality

2024–25 · Essay

Analyzed Cuba's 2019 constitutional reforms, arguing the Cuban state systematically prioritizes equality over freedom. Constitutional rights are granted to LGBTQ+ individuals, women, and racial minorities, but exist as conditional freedoms revocable when they conflict with Communist Party interests. Draws on the Cato Institute's Human Freedom Index, Martha Minow on equality, and empirical data from CubaData surveys.

Francophone Cinema and its Articulations · Honors

Ethical Ambiguity in The Battle of Algiers

Honors Assignment · Essay

Analyzed Gillo Pontecorvo's 1966 film through Hannah Arendt and Frantz Fanon, arguing it refuses to moralize by presenting political violence as a cyclical, dialectical force shaped by colonial power structures. Pontecorvo's cinematic symmetry, depicting French torture and FLN bombings with equal unflinching clarity, forces viewers to hold moral complexity without resolution, a quality contrasted against contemporary American blockbuster cinema and connected to the US Iraq invasion, Myanmar's civil war, and Gaza campus protests.

Irish Language Politics & Fontaines D.C.

2024–25 · Essay

A critical discourse analysis of Fontaines D.C.'s In ár gCroíthe go deo (Skinty Fia, 2022), examined against simultaneous UK suppression and Irish government revival efforts for the Irish language. 2024–25 · Research Design (Draft)

Proposed a within-case analysis of why Algeria's FLN leadership established a one-party authoritarian state despite emerging from a popular struggle for self-determination.
